# Tomato_Articles
Tomato Zn stress effect on tolerant vs resistant genotypes

**Zinc (Zn) stress responses in tomato: genotype-dependent effects in a sensitive vs. a tolerant genotype**

This repository contains the manuscript and final figures of an original research article prepared for *Plant Physiology and Biochemistry* (PPB), investigating the physiological and biochemical responses of two tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum* L.) genotypes — **Heinz (Zn-sensitive)** and **ST-P (Zn-tolerant)** — to zinc stress.

## 📋 Study overview

- **Genotypes:** Heinz (sensitive) × ST-P (tolerant)
- **Treatments:** 3 Zn levels applied as ZnSO₄·7H₂O — Zn0 (control), Zn100 (100 mg·kg⁻¹) and Zn250 (250 mg·kg⁻¹)
- **Design:** randomized blocks — 3 blocks × 2 genotypes × 3 treatments = 18 experimental units (3 plants per unit as subsamples; 54 plants in total)
- **Measured traits:** morphology and biomass, water status (RWC), soluble proteins, Zn accumulation in leaves and roots, photosynthetic pigments (chlorophyll a, b, carotenoids) and peroxidase (POD) activity
- **Key findings:** Zn stress drives changes in proteins, Zn accumulation, chlorophyll and POD with clear genotype-dependent responses and significant genotype × Zn interactions. Heinz follows a root-retention **exclusion strategy** (TF < 1, low basal POD, strong photosynthetic damage), whereas ST-P follows a **compartmentation strategy** (efficient translocation TF > 1, high constitutive POD, vacuolar sequestration, protein maintenance). Constitutive POD activity, translocation factor (TF) and protein maintenance at high [Zn] emerge as exploitable biomarkers.
